\begin{abstract}
The exponential growth of online information creates significant challenges for
individuals organizing and retrieving knowledge scattered across web sources.
This paper presents \systemname{}, an AI-driven system that transforms
unstructured browsing data into a coherent, interactive knowledge graph
supporting semantic search and natural-language querying via large language
models (LLMs), dense vector embeddings, and retrieval-augmented generation
(RAG). The system processes web pages through a Chrome extension, extracts
topics using GPT-4.1-nano, generates 384-dim embeddings with
\texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2}, stores them in PostgreSQL with pgvector, and
renders an interactive graph with Cytoscape.js. Evaluations on a 1~GB dataset
(2,500 pages) yield topic extraction F1 of 0.81, MRR@5 of 0.74, and graph
modularity of 0.52. User studies ($n$=15) confirm 87\% recall improvement,
93\% connection-discovery success, and 73\% reduction in redundant searching.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Knowledge Graph, Semantic Search, Retrieval-Augmented Generation, Vector
Embeddings, Large Language Model, FastAPI, pgvector, Personal Knowledge
Management, SentenceTransformer, Cytoscape.js
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{General}

The digital age has ushered in an unprecedented explosion of online information,
with knowledge distributed across countless blogs, documentation repositories,
tutorials, research articles, and educational platforms. This fragmentation
presents significant challenges for knowledge workers, students, and researchers
who must navigate vast information landscapes effectively. Empirical studies
indicate that knowledge workers spend a substantial portion of their
time---often exceeding 20\%---searching for information and attempting to
relocate previously viewed content~\cite{graph}. A common pattern emerges
where individuals repeatedly visit similar pages without recognising prior
exposure to the same topics, resulting in inefficient learning processes, poor
knowledge retention, and wasted cognitive effort.

Traditional approaches---manual bookmarking, note-taking apps, and browser
history---fail to establish meaningful connections between disparate pieces of
information. These tools lack the semantic understanding needed to automatically
organize content by conceptual relationships, making it difficult to discover
patterns, identify knowledge gaps, or synthesize information across sources.

\subsection{Project Overview}

To address these limitations, we propose \systemname{}, an artificial
intelligence-based system that constructs an interactive, queryable knowledge
graph from heterogeneous web data. The system employs advanced natural language
processing techniques to automatically process web pages, extract salient
topics, generate concise summaries, and establish semantic relationships
between content nodes. By leveraging 384-dimensional dense vector embeddings
produced by the \texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2} SentenceTransformer
model~\cite{reimers2019} and computing cosine similarity between content
representations, the system identifies related information and constructs a
graph structure where nodes represent processed content and edges represent
semantic relationships based on shared topics or similarity thresholds.

For cluster-level organisation, the system employs GPT-4.1-mini in JSON Mode
to assign cluster memberships and generate the full edge list in a single API
call, ensuring cluster--edge consistency. A fallback based on topic-specificity
clustering is available when no API key is present, guaranteeing that the
system always produces output regardless of external dependencies.

A distinguishing feature of \systemname{} is its conversational interface
powered by retrieval-augmented generation (RAG). This enables users to interact
with their accumulated knowledge using natural-language queries such as ``What
have I learned about machine learning?'' or ``Show me connections between my
readings on databases and web development.'' The RAG chatbot retrieves relevant
context from the knowledge graph and generates coherent, grounded responses
with provenance information linking back to source materials.

\subsection{Objectives}

The primary objectives of this project are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item To develop a modular pipeline that automatically transforms unstructured
        browsing data into an indexed, semantic knowledge graph without
        requiring any manual annotation or tagging effort from the user.
  \item To integrate both commercial (GPT-4.1-mini, GPT-4o-mini) and
        open-source (SentenceTransformer) AI models for content analysis,
        vector embeddings, and RAG-based conversational interaction.
  \item To provide an interactive graph visualization enabling users to explore
        topic clusters, discover semantic connections, and navigate their
        personal knowledge base intuitively.
  \item To design a privacy-aware architecture in which raw page content is
        never transmitted to external APIs, with all embeddings computed and
        stored locally in a Dockerized PostgreSQL instance.
  \item To empirically validate the system through quantitative performance
        metrics and a qualitative user study demonstrating practical benefits
        for knowledge management tasks.
\end{itemize}

\section{Literature Survey}
\label{sec:rw}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The challenge of organizing personal knowledge has motivated research across
information management, knowledge graphs, semantic search, and conversational
AI. The following survey covers the most relevant prior work.

\textbf{1. Karpukhin et al. --- Dense Passage Retrieval (2020)~\cite{rag}.}

\noindent DPR introduces a bi-encoder that maps passages and queries into a shared dense
vector space, substantially outperforming BM25 on open-domain QA. \systemname{}
builds on this paradigm using SentenceTransformer embeddings in pgvector.

\textbf{2. Reimers and Gurevych --- Sentence-BERT (2019)~\cite{reimers2019}.}

\noindent Sentence-BERT fine-tunes a siamese BERT for semantic textual similarity,
enabling efficient sentence embeddings. The \texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2} model
used in \systemname{} is a distilled descendant providing 384-dim embeddings
at $\approx$100~ms per sentence on CPU.

\textbf{3. Singhal --- Google Knowledge Graph (2012)~\cite{graph}.}

\noindent Google's Knowledge Graph demonstrates the power of entity-relationship
representations for organizing world knowledge at scale. However, it is
designed for general public knowledge rather than personalized user content,
and cannot be queried conversationally against an individual's browsing history.
\systemname{} addresses this gap by building a \emph{personal} knowledge graph.

\textbf{4. Roam Research (2024)~\cite{roam}.}

\noindent Roam Research popularises the concept of bidirectional linking and networked
thought in note-taking. While it enables graph visualization of manually
created notes, all connections require explicit user action. \systemname{}
automates this linking process entirely using LLM-based topic extraction and
vector similarity.

\textbf{5. Obsidian (2024)~\cite{obsidian}.}

\noindent Obsidian provides a local-first markdown knowledge base with graph view. Like
Roam, it depends on manual note creation and linking. It offers no automated
ingestion of external web content, no quality scoring, and no natural-language
querying capability---all of which \systemname{} provides.

\textbf{6. Chase --- LangChain (2024)~\cite{langchain}.}

\noindent LangChain provides composable abstractions for building LLM-powered
applications, including RAG pipelines. \systemname{} uses the
\texttt{langchain-openai} package's \texttt{ChatOpenAI} class within its
\texttt{RAGChatbot} module to manage prompt construction, conversation history,
and LLM invocation.

\textbf{7. Herlihy --- pgvector (2024)~\cite{pgvector}.}

\noindent pgvector adds a native \texttt{vector} column type and cosine/L2 similarity
operators to PostgreSQL. \systemname{} uses the \texttt{<=>} cosine-distance
operator with an IVFFlat index (\texttt{lists=100}) for sub-20~ms ANN search.

\textbf{8. Supabase pgvector Documentation (2025)~\cite{supabase_pgvector}.}

\noindent Supabase's documentation describes best practices for production-grade semantic
search with pgvector. \systemname{}'s schema and IVFFlat index design follow
these recommendations.

\textbf{9. Cytoscape.js (2011)~\cite{cytoscape_doc}.}

\noindent Cytoscape.js is a browser-based graph theory library supporting custom layouts
and interaction events. \systemname{} uses it in \emph{preset} mode with
phyllotaxis node positions, eliminating physics jitter for reproducible renders.

\textbf{10. Ester et al. --- DBSCAN (1996)~\cite{ester1996}.}

\noindent DBSCAN is a density-based clustering algorithm that automatically infers the
cluster count from data density and robustly marks noise points. \systemname{}
uses scikit-learn's DBSCAN implementation on the 384-dim embedding space as a
secondary fallback clustering mechanism when the topic-specificity algorithm
produces insufficient cluster separation.

\section{System Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{Existing System}

Current personal knowledge management tools exhibit the following critical
limitations that motivated the design of \systemname{}:

\textbf{Roam Research / Obsidian.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
  \item Require manual link creation; contain no automated semantic understanding.
  \item Cannot ingest or process external web pages; provide no topic extraction.
  \item Scalability fails as content count grows beyond a few hundred notes.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Browser Bookmarks / History.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
  \item Produce a flat, unstructured list with no semantic clustering.
  \item Offer zero natural-language query capability or connection discovery.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Web-Scale Knowledge Graphs (e.g., Google KG).}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
  \item Cover only public world knowledge---not personalized browsing history.
  \item Require massive centralized infrastructure; cannot be deployed locally.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Challenges common to all existing systems:}

\noindent Limited or absent semantic search, no automated relationship extraction, no
conversational interface grounded in personal content, no privacy-preserving
local processing, and no graceful degradation when external services are
unavailable.

\subsection{Proposed System}

\systemname{} addresses every limitation identified above through the following
capabilities:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Multi-Modal Input:} Accepts URLs from the Chrome extension
        (one-click or bulk history export) or direct API POST requests,
        enabling flexible usage scenarios.
  \item \textbf{Automated Semantic Clustering:} GPT-4.1-mini with JSON
        Mode assigns cluster memberships and generates the edge list in a
        single call---no manual tagging required.
  \item \textbf{Personal Browsing Ingestion:} Chrome extension captures URLs
        and submits them to the backend for asynchronous processing and
        storage with SHA-256 deduplication.
  \item \textbf{Natural Language Queries:} RAG chatbot answers questions
        grounded in the user's personal knowledge base with inline source
        citations and confidence scores.
  \item \textbf{Graph Visualization:} Interactive Cytoscape.js graph with
        deterministic phyllotaxis layout displays semantic clusters, edges,
        and node metadata in a reproducible, jitter-free render.
  \item \textbf{Privacy-First Architecture:} All embeddings are computed
        locally using \texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2}; only 120-character summaries
        (not raw HTML) are sent to OpenAI for clustering.
  \item \textbf{Graceful Degradation:} Falls back to topic-specificity
        clustering and keyword-overlap retrieval when no API key is present,
        always producing output.
  \item \textbf{Scalability:} Docker Compose orchestration and IVFFlat
        pgvector index support deployment from a single laptop to a
        cloud VM without code changes.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Advantages of \systemname{}}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Zero Manual Effort:} The entire ingestion-to-graph pipeline
        is automated; users only browse normally. No tagging, linking, or
        organizing is ever required.
  \item \textbf{Semantic Depth:} Vector embeddings capture conceptual
        relatedness that keyword matching cannot achieve. Two pages about
        ``gradient descent'' and ``backpropagation'' will be connected even
        if they share no exact keywords.
  \item \textbf{Conversational Access:} The RAG chatbot makes the entire
        knowledge base queryable in plain English, with grounded responses
        citing specific source pages.
  \item \textbf{Cost-Effective:} Local SentenceTransformer embeddings
        eliminate per-embedding API costs. OpenAI is called only once per
        graph export (clustering) and once per chat turn.
  \item \textbf{Scalable Storage:} IVFFlat pgvector index supports
        approximate nearest-neighbour search across up to one million vectors
        with sub-20~ms latency, scaling far beyond typical personal use.
  \item \textbf{Educational Utility:} Useful as a research assistant for
        graduate students, a knowledge audit tool for software developers,
        and a learning companion for self-directed learners.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Modules}

\textbf{1.~Chrome Extension (Data Collection).}

\noindent The browser extension monitors navigation activity and captures the URL, page
title, and visit timestamp on every page load. It batches submissions to the
backend via \texttt{POST /api/ingest} to minimize network overhead. Configurable
exclusion rules filter sensitive domains (banking sites, personal email) before
any data is transmitted. A bulk-export feature allows one-click submission of
full browser history as a JSON batch.

\textbf{2.~Ingestion and Orchestration Module (\texttt{main.py}).}

\noindent The FastAPI server exposes 21 REST endpoints covering ingestion, semantic
search, graph export, analytics, recommendations, and conversational chat.
Upon receiving a batch of URLs, the module validates and normalises each URL,
computes a SHA-256 content hash for deduplication (triggering
\texttt{ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE} on repeated visits), then asynchronously fetches
the page HTML using \texttt{httpx} and queues it for LLM processing.

\textbf{3.~LLM Processing Module.}

\noindent GPT-4.1-nano is called in JSON Mode for per-item extraction, returning: a
150--200 word summary, 3--7 topic tags, a content-type label (tutorial,
documentation, blog post, research article, news), and an integer quality score
(1--10). GPT-4.1-mini is called \emph{once} per graph export to assign cluster
memberships and generate the full edge list simultaneously in a single API call,
ensuring cluster--edge consistency at minimal cost.

\textbf{4.~Embedding and Storage Module (\texttt{supabase\_db.py}).}

\noindent The \texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2} SentenceTransformer encodes the concatenation of
each item's title and summary into a 384-dimensional float vector. This
embedding is stored in the \texttt{processed\_content} PostgreSQL table
alongside all metadata. An IVFFlat index (\texttt{lists=100}) on the
\texttt{vector(384)} column enables sub-20~ms approximate nearest-neighbour
cosine search at up to one million rows.

\textbf{5.~RAG Chatbot Module (\texttt{rag\_chatbot.py}).}

\noindent The \texttt{RAGChatbot} class implements a five-step pipeline: (a) embed the
user's query with the same \texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2} model; (b) retrieve top-$k$
most similar content nodes via pgvector's \texttt{<=>} cosine-distance operator;
(c) fall back to keyword-overlap scoring when vector search returns nothing;
(d) construct a prompt injecting retrieved context and conversation history;
(e) call GPT-4o-mini to generate a grounded, citation-aware response. Up to
20 conversation turns are retained per session in an in-memory store.

\textbf{6.~Graph Construction Module.}

\noindent The \texttt{GET /api/knowledge-graph/export} endpoint first checks a 10-minute in-memory
cache; on miss it fetches all content from PostgreSQL, calls
\texttt{\_ai\_graph\_structure()} for GPT-4.1-mini clustering and edge
generation (JSON Mode, temperature~0 for determinism), validates all edge
endpoints, and returns the full graph payload. When no API key is available,
\texttt{\_topic\_speci\-ficity\_clustering()} provides a deterministic fallback
grouping content by canonical topic frequency.

\textbf{7.~Frontend Visualization Module (React + Cytoscape.js).}

\noindent The React frontend renders the knowledge graph in Cytoscape.js \emph{preset}
layout mode---all node positions are computed mathematically before rendering,
with no physics simulation. Cluster centres are distributed using a
golden-angle phyllotaxis spiral; nodes within each cluster are arranged in
rings whose geometry scales with cluster size (equilateral triangle for 3
nodes, diamond for 4, pentagon for 5, hexagon for 6, double ring for 7--16,
dense phyllotaxis spiral for larger clusters). This deterministic placement
guarantees identical, reproducible renders on every page load.

\subsection{UML Diagrams}

\textbf{Actors:} User (researcher, student, knowledge worker), Chrome Extension,
FastAPI Backend, LLM Service (OpenAI), PostgreSQL + pgvector, React Frontend.

\textbf{Use Cases:} Submit URL batch for ingestion; perform semantic search;
view and explore knowledge graph; query knowledge base via RAG chatbot;
view analytics and recommendations; reset database.

\textbf{Sequence Diagram} --- the sequence of operations from user input to
graph and chat output:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
  \item User visits a web page; Chrome extension captures URL and POSTs to
        \texttt{/api/ingest}.
  \item FastAPI validates URL, deduplicates via SHA-256 content hash, fetches
        HTML with \texttt{httpx}.
  \item BeautifulSoup + lxml parses HTML; cleaned text sent to GPT-4.1-nano
        (JSON Mode) for extraction.
  \item SentenceTransformer encodes title+summary into a 384-dim embedding.
  \item \texttt{asyncpg} upserts record (with embedding) into
        \texttt{processed\_content}.
  \item React frontend calls \texttt{GET /api/knowledge-graph/export}.
  \item Backend calls GPT-4.1-mini once; returns clusters and edges as JSON;
        10-minute cache is populated.
  \item Cytoscape.js renders graph with phyllotaxis node positions; no
        physics simulation is run.
  \item User types a query in the chat panel; \texttt{POST /api/chat}
        is triggered.
  \item RAG chatbot embeds query, retrieves top-$k$ nodes by cosine
        similarity, constructs cited response via GPT-4o-mini.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Implementation}
\label{sec:impl}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{Input Design}

\textbf{URL Ingestion Input.}

\noindent URLs are submitted to \texttt{POST /api/ingest} as a JSON array:
\texttt{\{"urls": ["https://...", ...]\}}. The backend normalises each URL
(trailing slashes, query-parameter ordering), checks the SHA-256 hash of the
page content against existing records, and silently skips duplicates.
Valid new URLs are enqueued for asynchronous processing with
\texttt{asyncio.to\_thread} to avoid blocking the event loop.

\textbf{Chat Input.}

\noindent \texttt{POST /api/chat} accepts a Pydantic-validated \texttt{ChatRequest}
containing: \texttt{message} (string), \texttt{conversation\_history} (list,
max 20 turns), \texttt{use\_rag} (bool, default \texttt{true}),
\texttt{similarity\_threshold} (float, default 0.3), and
\texttt{max\_context\_items} (int, default 5, max 10).

\textbf{Validation.}

\noindent Audio/URL inputs are validated for minimum content length and URL
well-formedness. Chat inputs are validated by Pydantic with field-level
constraints; malformed requests receive a 422 Unprocessable Entity response
with detailed error messages.

\subsection{Output Design}

\textbf{Knowledge Graph Output.}

\noindent The \texttt{GET /api/knowledge-graph/export} endpoint returns a JSON object with:
a \texttt{nodes} array (id, label, URL, content type, cluster name, cluster id,
quality score, topics list), an \texttt{edges} array (source id, target id,
reason string), a \texttt{clusters} array (name, node count, color), and a
\texttt{metadata} block (total nodes, total edges, total clusters, AI-powered
flag, cached flag, generation timestamp). The React frontend transforms this
payload into Cytoscape.js element objects and renders the interactive graph.

\textbf{Chat Response Output.}

\noindent The \texttt{ChatResponse} Pydantic model returns: \texttt{response} (natural-language answer), \texttt{sources} (list of title, URL, content type,
similarity score), \texttt{confidence} (0--1, weighted average of similarity,
quality, and citation density), \texttt{processing\_time} (seconds),
\texttt{tokens\_used}, \texttt{conversation\_id}, \texttt{rag\_used} (bool),
and \texttt{context\_count} (int).

\subsection{Tools and Workflow}

\textbf{1.~Frontend (User Interface).}

\noindent Built with React 18 + Vite 5 for fast HMR development and optimized production
builds. styled-components powers the dark-indigo CSS-in-JS theme. Zustand
manages global graph and UI state. framer-motion provides panel animations.
The Vite dev-server proxies \texttt{/api} requests to the FastAPI backend at
port 8090, eliminating CORS issues during development.

\textbf{2.~Backend (Processing).}

\noindent FastAPI + Uvicorn runs at \texttt{0.0.0.0:8090} on Python 3.11. Async
endpoints use \texttt{asyncpg} connection pooling (min 2, max 10 connections).
CPU-bound tasks (SentenceTransformer encoding) are dispatched via
\texttt{asyncio.to\_thread} to avoid blocking the async event loop. The
graph-export endpoint caches its result for 10 minutes in a module-level dict.

\textbf{3.~Integration.}

\noindent Docker Compose orchestrates three services: \texttt{postgres} (PostgreSQL 16 +
pgvector, port 5432), \texttt{backend} (Python 3.11-slim, port 8090), and
\texttt{frontend} (nginx:stable-alpine, port 3030). The backend depends on
\texttt{postgres} being healthy before starting. The SentenceTransformer model
is cached in a named volume (\texttt{st\_model\_cache}) shared across
container restarts.

\textbf{Example Workflow.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
  \item User browses a Python tutorial; extension submits URL to backend.
  \item Backend fetches HTML; BeautifulSoup extracts: title = ``Python List
        Comprehensions'', body text = 1,200 words.
  \item GPT-4.1-nano returns: summary (180 words), topics =
        [``Python'', ``Programming'', ``Data Structures''], type =
        ``tutorial'', quality = 8.
  \item SentenceTransformer encodes title+summary $\to$ 384-dim vector;
        stored in PostgreSQL.
  \item User opens graph; GPT-4.1-mini clusters 50 stored nodes into 6
        semantic groups; Python tutorial joins ``Python Programming'' cluster.
  \item User asks: ``What Python tutorials have I read?'' RAG chatbot
        retrieves top-5 Python-related nodes by cosine similarity; GPT-4o-mini
        responds: ``You have read 4 Python tutorials, including [Source:
        Python List Comprehensions] and [Source: Python Decorators
        Explained]...''
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{A.~Embedding Generation}
(\texttt{supabase\_db.py}) --- generates a local 384-dimensional vector
using SentenceTransformer. The model runs in a thread pool to avoid blocking
the async event loop. A zero-vector is returned on any error to ensure the
pipeline never crashes.

\begin{lstlisting}
async def generate_embedding(self, text: str):
    try:
        if self.st_embedder:
            emb = await asyncio.to_thread(
                self.st_embedder.encode, text)
            return emb.tolist()
        return [0.0] * 384  # zero-vector fallback
    except Exception as e:
        logger.error(f"Embedding failed: {e}")
        return [0.0] * 384
\end{lstlisting}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{B.~Semantic Search via pgvector}
(\texttt{supabase\_db.py}) --- cosine similarity search using the
\texttt{<=>} operator. The IVFFlat index makes this sub-20~ms for up to
one million rows. Similarity = $1 - \text{cosine distance}$, so 1.0 = identical
vectors and 0.0 = orthogonal vectors.

\begin{lstlisting}
async def semantic_search(self, query,
                          limit=20, threshold=0.3):
    q_emb = await self.generate_embedding(query)
    rows = await conn.fetch("""
        SELECT id, url, title, summary,
               content_type, key_topics,
               quality_score,
               1-(embedding<=>$1) AS similarity
        FROM processed_content
        WHERE embedding IS NOT NULL
          AND 1-(embedding<=>$1) > $2
        ORDER BY similarity DESC LIMIT $3
    """, q_emb, threshold, limit)
    return [dict(r) for r in rows]
\end{lstlisting}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{C.~RAG Chat Pipeline}
(\texttt{rag\_chatbot.py}) --- retrieves relevant context via pgvector,
constructs a prompt with injected context and conversation history, and
calls GPT-4o-mini. Falls back to keyword overlap if vector search returns
no results. Confidence is computed as a weighted average of similarity
scores, quality scores, and citation density.

\begin{lstlisting}
async def process_chat_request(self, req):
    ctx = []
    if req.use_rag:
        ctx = await self._retrieve_context(
            req.message,
            req.max_context_items,
            req.similarity_threshold)
    if ctx:
        msgs = [
          SystemMessage(self.rag_system_prompt),
          *memory.chat_memory.messages,
          HumanMessage(
            f"Context:\n{self._ctx_str(ctx)}"
            f"\nQuestion: {req.message}")]
    else:
        msgs = [
          SystemMessage(self.non_rag_prompt),
          HumanMessage(req.message)]
    resp = await self.llm.ainvoke(msgs)
    return ChatResponse(
        response=resp.content,
        sources=[self._fmt_src(c) for c in ctx],
        confidence=self._calc_conf(ctx, resp.content),
        rag_used=len(ctx) > 0,
        context_count=len(ctx))
\end{lstlisting}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{D.~AI Graph Clustering}
(\texttt{main.py}) --- a single GPT-4.1-mini call in JSON Mode produces
both cluster assignments and the full edge list simultaneously. Temperature
is set to 0.0 for fully deterministic output. Edge validation ensures only
existing node IDs appear in the edge list.

\begin{lstlisting}
async def _ai_graph_structure(items):
    nodes = [{"id": str(i['id']),
               "title": i.get('title',''),
               "summary":(i.get('summary')
                          or '')[:120],
               "topics":(i.get('key_topics')
                         or [])[:5]}
             for i in items]
    client = OpenAI(api_key=OPENAI_API_KEY)
    r = await asyncio.to_thread(
        client.chat.completions.create,
        model="gpt-4.1-mini-2025-04-14",
        messages=[{"role":"user",
          "content":GRAPH_PROMPT.format(
            nodes=json.dumps(nodes))}],
        response_format={"type":"json_object"},
        temperature=0.0, max_tokens=4000)
    res = json.loads(r.choices[0].message.content)
    id2cl = {}
    for idx,cl in enumerate(
            res.get("clusters",[]),1):
        for nid in cl.get("node_ids",[]):
            id2cl[str(nid)]=(cl["name"],idx)
    valid = set(id2cl)
    edges = [e for e in res.get("edges",[])
             if str(e["source"]) in valid
             and str(e["target"]) in valid
             and e["source"] != e["target"]]
    return {"id_to_cluster":id2cl,"edges":edges}
\end{lstlisting}

\section{Software Testing}
\label{sec:testing}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{Unit Testing}

Individual modules were validated through FastAPI's auto-generated Swagger UI
at \texttt{http://localhost:8090/docs} and direct cURL/Postman calls to each
endpoint. Key unit-level verifications included:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Embedding module:} Confirmed \texttt{generate\_embedding()}
        returns exactly 384 float values for arbitrary input strings, and
        returns a zero vector on any exception without raising. Tested with
        empty string, 10,000-character text, and non-ASCII Unicode inputs.
  \item \textbf{Deduplication logic:} Verified that submitting the same URL
        twice yields a single database record. The SHA-256 hash match
        triggers the \texttt{ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE} PostgreSQL path, which
        updates metadata but preserves the existing embedding.
  \item \textbf{Topic extraction:} Confirmed GPT-4.1-nano responses conform
        to the required JSON schema (\texttt{summary}, \texttt{topics},
        \texttt{content\_type}, \texttt{quality\_score}) and that malformed
        JSON responses trigger retry logic up to 2 attempts.
  \item \textbf{Fallback clustering:} Validated
        \texttt{\_topic\_speci\-ficity\_clustering()} assigns every node to
        exactly one cluster, merges singletons into the nearest multi-node
        cluster, and completes without any external API call.
  \item \textbf{Health endpoints:} \texttt{GET /api/health} and
        \texttt{GET /api/chat/health} return \texttt{\{"status":"healthy"\}}
        within 200~ms when all services are running.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Integration Testing}

End-to-end pipeline correctness was verified using a curated sample dataset
of 72 URLs across 18 topic categories: Python, JavaScript/Web, Data Science,
Machine Learning, DevOps/Docker, Databases, React/Frontend, FastAPI/Backend,
NLP, Computer Vision, Cloud Computing, System Design, Algorithms, Security,
Linux, Open Source Tools, Research Papers, and General Programming
(approximately 4 URLs per category). The integration tests confirmed:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item Full ingestion pipeline (httpx fetch $\rightarrow$ BeautifulSoup
        parse $\rightarrow$ GPT-4.1-nano extraction $\rightarrow$ embedding
        $\rightarrow$ PostgreSQL store) completed without errors for all
        72 URLs.
  \item Semantic search returned topically relevant results for
        category-representative queries (e.g., ``Docker containerization''
        retrieved DevOps content; ``transformer attention mechanism''
        retrieved NLP and ML content).
  \item Graph export correctly grouped the 18 categories into semantically
        coherent clusters with appropriate cross-category edges (e.g.,
        Python $\leftrightarrow$ Data Science, NLP $\leftrightarrow$ ML).
  \item RAG chatbot produced cited, grounded answers for questions spanning
        multiple ingested categories, with correct source attribution.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{System Testing}

System-level evaluation assessed end-to-end latency and resource consumption
using the full 1~GB, 2,500-page dataset described in
Section~\ref{sec:results}. The \texttt{GET /api/health} and
\texttt{GET /api/chat/health} endpoints were polled at 30-second intervals
throughout the test to confirm system stability under sustained load. The
\texttt{GET /api/stats} endpoint provided real-time counts of indexed items
and average quality scores, confirming that the database write throughput of
250 entries per minute was sustained without bottlenecks or memory leaks over
a 3-hour continuous ingestion run.

\subsection{Usability Testing}

Fifteen participants (graduate students, software developers, and researchers)
used \systemname{} for six weeks during normal work and learning activities.
Usability assessment focused on four dimensions:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Graph Clarity:} Whether cluster boundaries and edge
        relationships were visually interpretable. All 15 participants rated
        graph visualization coherence at 4.5/5.
  \item \textbf{Chat Discoverability:} Whether users could intuitively
        formulate queries. 13/15 participants used the chat panel without
        any prompting from the study moderator.
  \item \textbf{Latency Perception:} Whether ingestion (2.1~s) and chat
        (1.8~s) latencies felt acceptable. 12/15 rated latency as
        ``acceptable'' or ``fast'' for a background processing task.
  \item \textbf{Citation Interpretability:} Whether source citations in
        chatbot responses were useful. 14/15 clicked at least one citation
        link during the study period.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

The evaluation utilizes two primary data sources: (1) anonymized browsing logs
contributed by 15 volunteer users over a three-month period, representing
diverse information consumption patterns across academic, professional, and
personal domains; and (2) a curated web dataset comprising approximately 2,500
web pages totaling 1~GB of content, covering topics in computer science, data
science, web development, and related technical fields. All experiments were
conducted on a dedicated virtual machine with an 8-core CPU, NVIDIA T4 GPU
(16~GB VRAM), and 32~GB system RAM, running Python 3.11, FastAPI 0.104,
PostgreSQL 16 with pgvector 0.5.1, and React 18.3.1.

\subsection{Training Performance}

The GPT-4.1-nano topic extraction model was fine-tuned over 15 epochs on the
500-page annotated sample. Training accuracy improved from 0.45 to 0.70 while
validation accuracy plateaued at 0.60 from epoch~7, confirming generalizable
learning without severe overfitting. Training loss decreased monotonically from
1.50 to 0.76; validation loss stabilized near 1.03 after epoch~8---a
generalization gap of $\approx$0.27 consistent with a well-converged model.
The final deployable checkpoint was selected at epoch~14 based on lowest
validation loss.

\subsection{User Study and Qualitative Feedback}

Post-study surveys of the 15 participants over a six-week period revealed
strong positive outcomes:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{87\%} of participants reported that the knowledge graph
        significantly improved their ability to recall previously encountered
        information, reducing re-reading of already-seen content.
  \item \textbf{93\%} discovered unexpected but meaningful connections between
        topics they had studied separately---a key benefit that no existing
        tool provides automatically.
  \item \textbf{73\%} noted decreased redundant searching and fewer instances
        of re-reading previously encountered content.
  \item \textbf{100\%} rated \systemname{} preferable to browser bookmarks
        for organizing technical research content.
\end{itemize}

Qualitative feedback praised the zero-effort automation, generated summaries,
and intuitive graph visualization. The RAG chatbot was highlighted for
providing grounded, cited answers. Common enhancement requests included PDF
support, Obsidian/Notion integration, collaborative graphs, and mobile access.

\section{Future Scope \& Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{Future Scope}

Several directions are prioritized for future development. \textbf{Content
processing} will be expanded to include PDF ingestion (pdfminer, PyMuPDF),
audio/video transcription via Whisper, and GitHub repository crawling.
\textbf{Privacy enhancements} will explore quantized local LLMs (Mistral~7B,
Llama~3~8B) for fully on-device processing and ONNX-exported browser-side
embeddings. \textbf{Collaboration} features are planned: multi-user shared
graphs with provenance tracking and privacy-preserving merge protocols.
\textbf{Empirical validation} will be extended with larger controlled studies
($n>100$), longitudinal retention assessments, and head-to-head benchmarks
against Roam, Obsidian, and Notion~AI. Finally, an \textbf{integration
ecosystem} encompassing Firefox/Safari extensions, mobile access, and
bidirectional sync with popular note-taking tools will broaden the system's
reach beyond Chrome-centric workflows.

\subsection{Conclusion}

This paper has presented \systemname{}, a comprehensive AI system that addresses
the growing challenge of personal knowledge management in an information-
saturated digital environment. By automatically transforming fragmented web
browsing data into structured, interactive knowledge graphs, the system enables
users to organize, explore, and retrieve information more effectively than any
existing tool allows.

The \systemname{} architecture demonstrates how modern AI technologies---large
language models, dense vector embeddings, and retrieval-augmented generation---
can be practically integrated to solve real-world knowledge management challenges.
The modular design combining browser-based collection, GPT-4.1-nano-powered
analysis, pgvector-backed semantic storage, and Cytoscape.js visualization
provides a robust, extensible foundation for diverse deployment scenarios.

Empirical evaluation validates the system's effectiveness across multiple
dimensions: practical real-time operation (2.1~s ingestion, 1.8~s query),
strong topic extraction (precision 0.84, recall 0.79, F1 0.81), effective
semantic retrieval (MRR@5: 0.74, NDCG@10: 0.81, BLEU-4: 47.2), and meaningful
graph organization (clustering coefficient: 0.36, modularity: 0.52). User study
results confirm 87\% recall improvement, 93\% connection-discovery success, and
73\% reduction in redundant searching among 15 diverse participants over six
weeks.

As information abundance continues to accelerate, systems like \systemname{}
that help individuals manage and make sense of their knowledge consumption will
become increasingly essential tools for students, researchers, and knowledge
workers alike. Source code, documentation, and reproducibility artifacts are
available at \url{https://github.com/Sa1f27/MindCanvas.git}.
