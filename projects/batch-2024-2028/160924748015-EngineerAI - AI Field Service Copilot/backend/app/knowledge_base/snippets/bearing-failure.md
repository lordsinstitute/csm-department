---
title: "Bearing Failure (Abnormal Noise)"
problem_tags: ["Noise"]
source: "EngineerAI KB"
---

**Symptom:** A distinct grinding, rumbling, or screeching noise localized to one bearing housing, often changing pitch with motor speed and sometimes accompanied by a burning-grease smell or a warm-to-hot housing at that specific bearing. Noise is typically continuous while running, unlike an intermittent rub.

**Likely cause:** Advanced bearing wear, loss of lubrication, water or particulate contamination of the grease, or a bearing that has brinelled (developed flat spots) from being stored or transported under load. Once a bearing's rolling elements or races are pitted, the resulting noise is caused by metal-to-metal contact rather than smooth rolling.

**How to confirm:** With power locked out, rotate the shaft by hand and listen/feel for grinding or notchy resistance at specific points in the rotation. A stethoscope or long screwdriver held to the ear and pressed against the bearing housing while the motor briefly runs can pinpoint which bearing is the source. Elevated housing temperature specifically at that bearing (versus the opposite end) corroborates the diagnosis.

**Fix:** Replace the failed bearing with the correct manufacturer part number. Inspect the shaft journal and housing bore for damage from the failed bearing before installing the new one; a scored journal may need sleeving. Repack with the specified grease and verify shaft rotates smoothly and quietly before returning to service.

**Parts/tools:** Replacement bearing, bearing puller, induction heater or press, mechanic's stethoscope, specified grease.
