---
title: "Bearing Wear"
problem_tags: ["Vibration"]
source: "EngineerAI KB"
---

**Symptom:** Vibration that develops gradually over weeks or months, often accompanied by a slight rise in bearing housing temperature and, in later stages, a faint rough or gritty feel when the shaft is turned by hand with power removed. Distinguish from imbalance by the presence of higher-frequency content, not just 1x RPM.

**Likely cause:** Loss of lubrication, contamination of the grease with dirt or moisture, or normal fatigue at end of bearing life. Worn rolling elements or a pitted race generate vibration at frequencies tied to the bearing's geometry (ball pass frequencies), which appear as a raised noise floor or multiple non-integer peaks in a vibration spectrum rather than a single clean 1x peak.

**How to confirm:** Compare vibration spectra from drive-end and non-drive-end bearings; a wear-damaged bearing shows elevated broadband vibration and possible ultrasonic/acoustic emission increase. Manually rotate the shaft with the motor de-energized and locked out — roughness, tight spots, or axial play beyond the bearing's rated clearance confirms wear.

**Fix:** Replace the affected bearing with the manufacturer-specified part number and fit. Clean the housing and shaft journal, press the new bearing on using proper induction heating or a hydraulic press (never hammer directly on the race), and repack with the specified grease grade.

**Parts/tools:** Replacement bearing (correct bore/OD/width), bearing puller, induction heater or hydraulic press, specified grease, dial indicator to check shaft runout post-installation.
