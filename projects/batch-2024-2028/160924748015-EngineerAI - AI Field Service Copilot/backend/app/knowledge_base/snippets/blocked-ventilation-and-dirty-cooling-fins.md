---
title: "Blocked Ventilation and Dirty Cooling Fins"
problem_tags: ["Overheating"]
source: "EngineerAI KB"
---

**Symptom:** Gradual rise in operating temperature over weeks or months even though load has not changed. Housing fins and shroud are visibly coated in dust, oil residue, or fibrous material. Cooling fan may sound muffled if debris has built up on the fan blades themselves.

**Likely cause:** The motor's external cooling fan pushes air across the finned housing to reject heat; when the fins, shroud, or fan blades accumulate dirt, oil mist, or lint, airflow drops sharply and the motor runs hotter for the same electrical load. This is especially common in dusty, oily, or fibrous industrial environments and is one of the most frequent and easily preventable overheating causes.

**How to confirm:** Visually inspect the fan shroud inlet and housing fins for caked-on debris. Compare surface temperature readings (infrared thermometer) at the fins near the fan end versus the opposite end — a large gradient suggests restricted airflow rather than an internal electrical fault. Confirm the fan itself spins freely and is not cracked or missing blades.

**Fix:** Lock out power, then remove the fan shroud and clean the fins with a soft brush or low-pressure compressed air (avoid high-pressure air directly into bearings/seals). Clean or replace the external fan if blades are damaged or heavily fouled. Reinstall the shroud, ensuring the air gap is not obstructed.

**Parts/tools:** Soft-bristle brush, low-pressure compressed air, replacement fan/shroud if damaged, infrared thermometer.
