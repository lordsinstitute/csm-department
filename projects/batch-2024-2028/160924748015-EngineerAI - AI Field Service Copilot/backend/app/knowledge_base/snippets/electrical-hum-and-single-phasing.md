---
title: "Electrical Hum and Single-Phasing"
problem_tags: ["Noise"]
source: "EngineerAI KB"
---

**Symptom:** A loud, deep electrical hum or buzz — distinctly different from mechanical grinding — often present as soon as the motor is energized. The motor may struggle to reach full speed, stall under load, or trip on overload shortly after starting. Hum may be accompanied by noticeably higher current draw.

**Likely cause:** One of the three supply phases has been lost (blown fuse, tripped single-pole breaker, loose terminal, or failed contactor pole) while the other two remain energized — known as single-phasing. The motor attempts to run on two phases, producing a strong magnetic hum and pulling excessive current on the remaining phases as it struggles to develop torque.

**How to confirm:** With appropriate PPE, measure voltage at the motor terminals phase-to-phase; a missing or near-zero reading on one leg confirms loss of that phase. Measure current on all three phases with a clamp meter — in single-phasing, the two energized phases typically show significantly elevated current compared to nameplate FLA. Check upstream fuses, breaker poles, and contactor contacts for the open phase.

**Fix:** Do not attempt to run the motor further on two phases — de-energize immediately to prevent winding damage. Replace the blown fuse, reset or replace the failed breaker pole, or repair the loose/failed contactor contact causing the open phase, then verify balanced three-phase voltage before restarting.

**Parts/tools:** Multimeter, clamp meter, replacement fuse or contactor as needed, appropriate electrical PPE.
