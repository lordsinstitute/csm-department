---
title: "Loose Mounting Bolts"
problem_tags: ["Vibration"]
source: "EngineerAI KB"
---

**Symptom:** Vibration that is irregular or "knocking" rather than smooth, sometimes changing character when the technician pushes down on the motor housing or base. May be accompanied by visible movement of the motor feet, fretting marks or rust streaks around the mounting holes, and audible rattling at low speed.

**Likely cause:** Mounting bolts have backed out due to vibration over time, an undersized or missing lock washer, an improperly torqued initial installation, or a cracked/degraded foundation pad. A motor that is not rigidly secured amplifies any small residual imbalance or misalignment into much larger vibration at the housing.

**How to confirm:** With the motor de-energized and locked out, attempt to check each mounting bolt with a torque wrench against the manufacturer's specified torque. Visually inspect for rust bleeding from under the base (a sign of movement), elongated bolt holes, or a cracked grout/foundation pad. A pry bar check for base movement while a helper watches the coupling can also confirm looseness.

**Fix:** Clean mounting surfaces, replace any damaged or corroded bolts and washers, and re-torque all bolts to specification in a cross pattern. If the foundation pad itself is cracked or degraded, it must be repaired or re-grouted before re-mounting, or the fix will not hold.

**Parts/tools:** Torque wrench, replacement bolts/lock washers as needed, pry bar, foundation grout (if applicable).
