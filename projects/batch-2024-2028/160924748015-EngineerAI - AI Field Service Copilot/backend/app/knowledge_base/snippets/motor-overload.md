---
title: "Motor Overload"
problem_tags: ["Overheating"]
source: "EngineerAI KB"
---

**Symptom:** Motor housing noticeably hotter than normal to the touch or by infrared thermometer, often paired with the thermal overload relay tripping after the motor has been running for some time under load. Motor may also sound like it is working harder than usual (labored hum) as load increases.

**Likely cause:** The driven equipment is demanding more torque than the motor's nameplate rating supports — from a mechanical binding downstream, an increase in process load, a clogged filter increasing fan resistance, or the motor being undersized for its application. Continuous operation above nameplate current accelerates winding insulation aging and raises stator temperature.

**How to confirm:** Measure running current on all three phases with a clamp meter and compare to the nameplate full-load amps (FLA). Current consistently above FLA under normal operating conditions confirms overload. Check the driven equipment (pump, fan, conveyor) for binding, worn bearings, or blockage that would raise torque demand independent of the motor itself.

**Fix:** Address the downstream mechanical cause first (clear blockage, free binding, service the driven equipment). If the load is legitimately at or near the motor's rating, verify the overload relay is set correctly per nameplate FLA and consider a motor upsizing if the application has grown beyond original sizing.

**Parts/tools:** Clamp meter, infrared thermometer, nameplate data sheet, overload relay setting chart.
