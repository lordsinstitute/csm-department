---
title: "Motor Shaft Misalignment"
problem_tags: ["Vibration"]
source: "EngineerAI KB"
---

**Symptom:** Vibration that increases with motor speed and is most pronounced at the coupling end. Technician may also notice premature coupling wear, elevated bearing temperature at the drive-end bearing, or a faint knocking at running speed.

**Likely cause:** The motor shaft and the driven equipment's shaft are not sharing a common centerline. Angular misalignment produces axial vibration; parallel (offset) misalignment produces radial vibration at 1x and 2x running speed. Common after re-installation, foundation settling, or coupling replacement without realignment.

**How to confirm:** Mount a dial indicator or laser alignment tool across the coupling. Rotate both shafts together and record readings at 0°, 90°, 180°, 270°. Total indicated runout beyond 0.05 mm (2 mils) at the coupling face or rim typically confirms misalignment. Vibration spectrum analysis showing dominant 1x and 2x peaks in the axial direction supports the finding.

**Fix:** Loosen motor mounting bolts, insert or remove shims under the motor feet to correct vertical offset, and adjust horizontal position with jacking bolts. Re-torque mounting bolts in a cross pattern once alignment is within 0.05 mm. Re-check alignment after the coupling is fully torqued, since clamping can shift position slightly.

**Parts/tools:** Dial indicator or laser alignment kit, shim stock (assorted thicknesses), torque wrench, feeler gauges.
