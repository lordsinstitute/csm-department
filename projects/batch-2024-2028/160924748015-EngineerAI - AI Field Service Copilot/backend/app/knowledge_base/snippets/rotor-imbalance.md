---
title: "Rotor Imbalance"
problem_tags: ["Vibration"]
source: "EngineerAI KB"
---

**Symptom:** Smooth, constant vibration that scales with motor speed and remains steady regardless of load. Unlike misalignment, imbalance vibration is predominantly radial and appears uniformly at all points around the housing rather than concentrated at the coupling.

**Likely cause:** Uneven mass distribution on the rotor — commonly from a missing or shifted balance weight, buildup of dirt or corrosion on fan blades, a bent shaft, or damage from a prior overspeed event. Imbalance produces a vibration signature dominant at exactly 1x running speed (1x RPM) with no significant harmonics.

**How to confirm:** Take a vibration reading with a handheld vibration meter or accelerometer at the motor's non-drive-end bearing housing in the radial direction. A spectrum showing a single sharp peak at 1x RPM, with phase readings roughly consistent across the housing, points to imbalance rather than misalignment or bearing wear. Visually inspect the cooling fan and rotor for debris, corrosion, or a visibly bent shaft.

**Fix:** Remove debris or corrosion from the fan and rotor surfaces. If imbalance persists, the rotor must be removed and dynamically balanced on a balancing machine, adding or removing weight until residual imbalance is within tolerance for the motor's speed class. Do not attempt field balancing without proper equipment.

**Parts/tools:** Vibration meter or accelerometer, balancing weight kit, rotor puller, dynamic balancing machine (offsite if unavailable).
