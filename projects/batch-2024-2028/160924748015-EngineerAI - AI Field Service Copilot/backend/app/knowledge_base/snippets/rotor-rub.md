---
title: "Rotor Rub"
problem_tags: ["Noise"]
source: "EngineerAI KB"
---

**Symptom:** An intermittent or continuous light scraping or rubbing sound from inside the motor housing, sometimes only present at certain speeds or when the motor is warm (and the rotor has thermally expanded). Distinct from bearing grinding in that the sound is a lighter, higher-pitched scrape rather than a coarse grind.

**Likely cause:** The rotor is making light contact with the stator bore, most often due to a worn bearing allowing the rotor to sag (reducing the air gap on one side), a bent shaft, or the rotor being off-center from a prior bearing failure that was replaced without checking rotor position. Even light contact produces a distinctive intermittent scrape as the rotor bars pass the contact point.

**How to confirm:** With power locked out, check radial bearing clearance by attempting to move the shaft up and down at the bearing housings — excessive play indicates the bearing that is allowing rotor sag. Visually inspect the rotor and stator bore (via end bell removal) for shiny rub marks or scoring, which confirm contact has occurred. A dial indicator check of shaft centerline relative to the stator bore can quantify air gap uniformity.

**Fix:** Replace any bearing found to have excessive play, and re-check that the rotor is centered in the stator bore afterward. If the shaft itself is bent, it must be straightened or replaced — do not return a bent-shaft motor to service, as rub will recur and can progress to a stator winding fault.

**Parts/tools:** Dial indicator, replacement bearings, feeler gauges for air-gap check.
