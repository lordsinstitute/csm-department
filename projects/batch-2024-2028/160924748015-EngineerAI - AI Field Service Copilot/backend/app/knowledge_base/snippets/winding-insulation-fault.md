---
title: "Winding Insulation Fault"
problem_tags: ["Overheating"]
source: "EngineerAI KB"
---

**Symptom:** Overheating that appears suddenly rather than gradually, sometimes with a faint burnt-varnish smell near the motor, discoloration on visible windings or terminal leads, or a slight increase in no-load current. In advanced cases the motor may trip on overload almost immediately after starting.

**Likely cause:** Insulation between winding turns or between windings and the frame has degraded from age, moisture ingress, repeated thermal cycling, or a prior overload event that cooked the varnish. Partial insulation breakdown causes localized shorted turns, which draw excess current and generate heat disproportionate to the mechanical load.

**How to confirm:** With the motor de-energized, isolated, and locked out, measure insulation resistance from each phase winding to ground using a megohmmeter (megger) at 500V DC for low-voltage motors. A reading below 1 megohm (or below the manufacturer's minimum) indicates a compromised winding. A burnt smell or visible dark discoloration on windings during a visual inspection further supports the finding. Compare phase-to-phase winding resistance; a significant imbalance (>5%) suggests shorted turns.

**Fix:** A confirmed winding fault requires the motor to be sent for rewinding or replaced outright — this is not a field-repairable fault. Tag the motor out of service, document the megger readings, and arrange rewind or replacement before returning it to duty.

**Parts/tools:** Megohmmeter (megger), digital multimeter, lockout-tagout kit, replacement or rewound motor.
